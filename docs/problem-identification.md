# Problem Identification

## Overview
Autonomous vehicles rely on artificial intelligence, sensors, software, and communication networks to operate safely. These technologies improve transportation but also introduce cybersecurity and AI-related risks that can affect safety, privacy, and reliability.

## Key Cybersecurity Risks

### Sensor Spoofing
Attackers may manipulate sensor inputs such as GPS, LiDAR, cameras, or radar, causing the vehicle to misinterpret its surroundings.

### Adversarial AI
Carefully crafted inputs can mislead AI models into making incorrect decisions, such as misclassifying traffic signs or obstacles.

### Data Manipulation
Unauthorized modification of vehicle or sensor data can lead to incorrect navigation and unsafe behavior.

### Communication Attacks
Vehicle-to-Vehicle (V2V) and Vehicle-to-Infrastructure (V2I) communications may be targeted through interception, tampering, or impersonation attacks.

### Software Vulnerabilities
Bugs, insecure configurations, and outdated software may allow attackers to gain unauthorized access to vehicle systems.

## Impact

These risks can result in:
- Unsafe driving decisions
- Loss of data integrity
- Privacy violations
- Service disruption
- Reduced public trust in autonomous vehicles

## Objective

The goal of this framework is to identify major cybersecurity and AI threats at an early stage and provide a foundation for effective risk assessment and mitigation.
